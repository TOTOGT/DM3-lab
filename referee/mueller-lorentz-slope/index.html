The Lorentz factor as the slope of an additional spatial coordinate: what the checks do and do not say

A. Mueller, The Lorentz Factor as the Slope of an Additional Spatial Coordinate, Zenodo v1 (16 Nov 2026), 62 pages. We checked whether the paper’s displayed algebra follows from its stated premises and whether its quoted numbers recompute. Every check we ran passed; the physical inputs are imported from general relativity, as the paper itself says.

Scope. This is not a review of the physics, and not an endorsement or refutation. Not read: §1 and §2.1 with Table 1, §2.3.6, §2.4.1–2.4.2, §4.1–4.5, Appendix A.1–A.3 and A.6, the Appendix B derivations, and the references. No rotation-curve data were available, so no χ² was recomputed. Prepared with Claude (Anthropic) assisting. Nothing here has been sent to the author.

Draft observations for the author (not sent)

  1. G44 = 1 follows from how y is defined. Since y is defined by ∂Ry = βγ, the Cassini bound constrains the construction as a whole rather than testing G44 independently.
  2. Eqs 46–47. G44(U) = 1 and G44(S)dy² = 0 are presented together; the second follows from the choice of the S frame, so it is a statement about that choice.
  3. Prop. 1 (the derived lapse). The ODE step is correct (Lean-checked). It is a derivation from the imported static vacuum equations, so any wording that the lapse is not postulated should keep that qualifier.
  4. Hypothesis M. With κ′ = 1/2π the deep-limit flat speed for the adopted baryonic mass is about 159 km/s (this ledger’s mass-normalisation estimate, not a fit) against the quoted 198–227 km/s. The paper’s own free fit gives g0/(cH0) = 0.35; reporting the χ² at 1/2π would show how much the factor of two matters.
  5. Appendix A, Earth. c√(GM/R³)·(1 s) recomputes to 372.2 km/s against 371.6 km/s quoted (0.16%). The match with the CMB dipole (369.82 km/s) is 0.64% and fixes the reference time at 0.9936 s; with 1 minute or 1 ms it fails. By the appendix’s own account this is a one-parameter fit.
  6. Equivalence to GR holds by import of the Hamiltonian constraint in the vacuum and pressureless spherical cases, not by derivation.
