DM3-lab

Contact geometry for dissipative systems that settle into structured cycles.

An independent research lab at G6 LLC in Newark, New Jersey. It works on the dm³ system, a class of dissipative dynamical systems with a hyperbolic limit cycle, and, separately, on a model of zeolite shape-selectivity that uses the same contact form: why two zeolites with the same acid chemistry select different products. Every claim on this page carries a status, and what is open is listed beside what is shown.

Origin

The dm³ system first appears in Generative Contact Mechanics (GCM). In that paper a dm³ system is a smooth Riemannian manifold with a hyperbolic limit cycle, a Lyapunov function and a stochastic extension, defined by eight axioms, with an operator algebra and an embedding in contact geometry. GCM is the lab's foundation text.

Why contact geometry: a Hamiltonian flow on a symplectic manifold preserves volume and cannot have an attracting limit cycle. A contact flow can dissipate. The same point appears below in a narrower form: a Reeb flow preserves contact volume and has no attracting set, so any confinement in the zeolite model has to come from somewhere else.

Work

Foundation text

Generative Contact Mechanics

Eight axioms, an operator algebra (g-, L-, R-, U-), and four stated theorems on existence, unification, a normal form and C¹ stability. The paper's own page says it was submitted to Journal of Geometric Mechanics in 2026; no acceptance is claimed here.

Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.20230610
Lean-checked core

The Self-Trapping Selectivity Principle

A model of why HZSM-5 and HMCM-22 diverge in ethanol-to-hydrocarbon selectivity, with a second case in Pt–Sn ensemble effects. The paper is deliberately narrow: what could not be derived was cut, and what is open is stated as open.

Version 6, 24 Sep 2026 · Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.22929142

Status ledger

What each claim is, and how it was checked. Verified means the Lean kernel accepted the statement as written, and a statement can be weaker than its name. Checked by script means a program ran a test that could fail. Derived means a complete pen-and-paper derivation, not formalized. Dates and counts are as recorded in the sources named in the last column.

Not proved: the continuum limit itself and the norm relation P = 2A²w/a. The older form a√(J/λ) is kept as the fixed-peak-amplitude case. io / paper section 4b–4c

In two- and three-dimensional cages (the MCM-22 supercage) the simulation shows a self-trapping threshold, not a width, so the smooth law should not be assumed there. io / scripts: fixed_norm_width_check.py, cage_threshold_check.py

The scale √(J/λ) is the generic crossover between spreading and localizing in many systems, so a shared functional form is not a shared invariant. The helical-phase prediction depended on Reeb orbits being helical, and they are not. io / paper, scope section

Check it yourself

The commands below are the ones the author used. They need your own toolchain, and a clean run says only that the file compiled on the day you ran it.

git clone https://github.com/TOTOGT/geometry
cd geometry
python3 gcm-framework-verify.py     # the Gibbs-form check and the corpus counts

lake build CatGT                    # the Lean file, under the toolchain in lean-toolchain
# then, in a file that imports it:
#   #print axioms <theorem name>     # expect only propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound

A passing check is not proof that a theorem says what its name says. Every new theorem here is read for what it states, not what it is called, before it is treated as settled.
