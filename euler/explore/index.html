2 · How close is the bound?

The open step needs, for every admissible pair (m, p), a number R to stay at or below 1. R is the one-vector ratio: the measured excess of returns to Op, divided by what the spectral bound allows. Each dot is one pair, computed exactly from Hurwitz quaternions of norm m.

prime p

Odd m < 9000 with every prime factor ≡ 3 (mod 4), p ∤ m. Filled dots satisfy the prime cutoff p4 ≤ 32m; hollow dots do not. Every tested pair has 2m < 18,000, far below 2m ≥ 644 where the bound is used. Exact verdict: R > 1 iff N > 0 and N2 > K2m (integers). Script: onevec.py.

3 · Where the eigenvalues sit

The spectral input says: on functions orthogonal to 1 and χp, every eigenvalue of the Hecke operator Tℓ lies in [−2√ℓ, 2√ℓ]. Each row is one prime ℓ, scaled so the allowed band is [−1, 1]. Diamonds are the two excluded eigenvalues, ℓ+1 and (ℓ/p)(ℓ+1).

level p

Operators built on Yp = Γp\PGL2(Fp), |Yp| = 28, 110, 570. All dots stay inside the band (largest 0.96 of the bound at p = 19). A finite check of a classical theorem, not a proof of it.

4 · The tail without the M(n) loss

A possible Deligne-free route bounds the large primes all at once instead of one by one. The prime-by-prime bound carries a factor M(n). Here is the actual tail sum over p above the Lean cutoff, against that bound.

show

3,037 values 1001 ≤ m < 20000, cutoff P = ⌊2n1/4⌋ (Lean Pn), tail over primes p ≡ 3 (mod 4) with P < p ≤ √n, including p | m. Weak evidence: M(n) is only 8–16 here. Script: tail.py.
