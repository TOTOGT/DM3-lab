What this chapter is for

The operator chain G = U∘F∘K∘C and the g-series are taxonomies. They give each phenomenon a place and an anchor, so that what is understood about a phenomenon in one domain or at one scale can be carried to another. They are not a new law of gravity. An entry in the chain is an anchor: the known structure on one side (a law, threshold, fold, measured quantity) and the operator slot it occupies on the other. Anchors are marked strong (the known side is checked), proposed (the slot reading is a proposal) or empty (no known structure written down yet).

The cut

This chapter: contact-geometric; axis: length, 10⁻³⁵ m to 10²² m, which is 57 orders of magnitude (the exponent difference). Other volumes cut the same material other ways (the first paper was symplectic; the Coherence Bridge pages speak of “17 orders of magnitude” across 12+ domains). The cuts nest by parity. Symplectic manifolds are even-dimensional and contact manifolds are odd-dimensional, and a contact manifold fibres over a symplectic base, the Reeb direction being the extra coordinate. This chapter’s (ℝ³, α) is the line bundle over the plane (x, y) with symplectic form dα = −2 dx∧dy: 3 = 2 + 1 (checked, sympy). Nothing can be dynamic without time, and the fibre coordinate z, twice the accumulated area, is a clock along each orbit; the dm³ tilde marks that role. The author’s statement is that the next case is 4 + t = 5: a four-dimensional (even, symplectic) base with time as the fibre, a five-dimensional (odd, contact) space, a modified Kaluza–Klein structure. Already in three dimensions the Sasakian metric of the box on Sasakian geometry below has Kaluza–Klein form, (dz − A)² + dx² + dy² with A = x dy − y dx, whose field strength F = dA = 2 dx∧dy is a uniform field on the base (checked). The five-dimensional case is not worked out here: which four-dimensional base, and whether its fibre coordinate is physical time, are open.

The dm³ tilde

The framework is named dm³, and its mark carries a tilde. The dm³ tilde says that the objects of study are in motion, which needs time: thresholds such as r*(z) and d/rH move as the system moves. A fold is not a place; it is where the system is heading or has passed.

The Central Conjecture

What is established here is the geometry of the contact condition itself. On the contact 3-manifold (ℝ³, α = dz − r²dθ), the condition α = 0 reads:

This holds at the Planck scale (where it encodes the Hopf fiber of the M-theory circle), at the nuclear scale (where it encodes the color confinement geometry of SU(3)), and at the electromagnetic scale (where it determines the stable levitation gap r* = 0.77594058). The same equation — three substrates — nobody talking to each other.

Sasakian Geometry: The Unifying Language

A manifold is Sasakian if its metric cone is Kähler. This is the odd-dimensional analog of Kähler geometry — and it is the natural home of the contact structure α = dz − r²dθ.

  • S³ is Sasakian. Its metric cone is ℂ² \ {0}, which is Kähler.
  • The dm³ manifold (ℝ³, α = dz − r²dθ) is a local model of a Sasakian 3-manifold.
  • Superstring compactifications on Sasakian manifolds (especially Sasaki-Einstein spaces S⁵, T^{1,1}) are the backbone of AdS/CFT.

From Cajueiros to Nebulas

The cajueiro (cashew tree) grows one seed — then overshoots, hits resistance, finds a new lock point, branches, and each branch becomes a new g⁶ seed. One tree becomes a forest. The pattern is the g-series cycle: seed → overshoot → resistance → lock → branch → new seed.

A nebula does the same thing at g⁹⁶. A gas cloud overshoots its self-gravity threshold, collapses, hits radiation pressure resistance, locks into a protostellar core at the Whitney fold, ignites, and the stellar wind seeds new molecular clouds — each a new g⁶ at cosmic scale. Geologists see it in fold mountains. Economists see it in market cycles. Mycologists see it in mycelium branching. Architects see it in load-path bifurcation.

Same pattern — different substrate — nobody talking. The g-series is the translation layer.

Added in v2 · how gravity folds and unfolds

Circular orbits around a mass M (G = c = 1) solve M r² − L² r + 3M L² = 0, whose discriminant is L²(L² − 12M²). The count of circular orbits is therefore two for L² > 12M², one at L² = 12M² (r = 6M), and none below. That is the standard fold normal form, and the chain reads it as:

OperatorReading
Cangular momentum lowered, the orbit set compressed
KVeff″ falls toward zero as r decreases to 6M
Fthe two circular orbits merge at r = 6M (A₁ fold of L²(r))
Uabove the fold a stable and an unstable orbit; below it, plunge

Unfolding. For L² > 12M² the pair is r± = (L² ± L√(L² − 12M²)) / 2M, stable outer and unstable inner. For large L, r+ → L²/M, which is Newton’s relation, and r− → 3M, the photon sphere. The unfolded state links the Newtonian regime to the photon sphere, and the fold at 6M is where the two branches meet. The fold lives in the space of orbits; it is not a statement that the field itself folds. Newton shows the case where the chain does not close, because L² = GM·r has no fold.
