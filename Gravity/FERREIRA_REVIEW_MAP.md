# Ferreira's review mapped onto "Gravity Across Scales"

Source reviewed: P. G. Ferreira, "Cosmological Tests of Gravity", Annual Review of Astronomy and Astrophysics (arXiv:1902.10503v3). Mapped to: P. N. Grossi, *Gravity Across Scales: Nano, Meso, and Macro Instantiations of the dm3 Operator Chain* (Principia Orthogona series, 2026).

Status of this note: prepared 20 Nov 2026, extended the same day with three further Downloads files (see the last section). The mapping uses the review's abstract, key-results list and section headings; it is not a section-by-section reading of the review's body. Nothing from the review is quoted or reproduced. Where a row says the paper "must" do something, that is an editorial suggestion for the author, not a claim about the physics.

## What the review does and does not touch

The review is about testing General Relativity on cosmological scales: how theories that extend GR are described, how screening hides modifications on small scales, and what large-scale-structure data constrain. The paper's mathematical results (T1 to T4: the limit cycle, the fold at r*, the saddle, the Sasakian structure) are about a dynamical system on a contact 3-manifold. They do not depend on the review, and the review neither supports nor contradicts them.

The link is at the paper's physical claims, which the paper already labels conjectured. There, the review works as a list of constraints and standards that a claim about gravity at a given scale would have to meet.

## Overall

The review gives the series a checklist for the physical half of the paper: say what is a model and what is a theory; say what is measured at each scale; say which observable would test the galactic conjecture. Adding those statements makes the conjectured claims more precise and does not change any proved result.

Mapped from the table of contents and the opening chapter; the later chapters on collapse and black holes were not read for this note, and nothing is quoted.
